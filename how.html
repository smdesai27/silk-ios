How it works

Silk: App Blocker, Screen Time

The apps you picked stay blocked. To use one, open Silk and ask for it.

Opening a blocked app

  1. Open Silk. A blocked app can't be opened from its own screen, and the OK button there only closes it.
  2. In the bar at the bottom, write the whole sentence: unlock Instagram for 10 min
  3. Stay on Silk while the circle draws. It takes a few seconds.
  4. The app opens. When the minutes run out, it locks again.

A request needs a verb like unlock, the app's name, and the minutes. If a part is missing, Silk writes out the full sentence for you to send. Undo, under Silk's reply, takes a request back.

The mic button opens the keyboard. To talk instead of type, use the keyboard's dictation key.

The wait

The wait only runs while Silk is on screen. If you leave, it pauses, and it picks up where it was when you come back. It is the price of opening an app, so it grows with what you ask for: about 3 seconds for 10 minutes, 18 for an hour.

Nothing comes off your budget until the wait ends. If you're gone for more than two minutes, the request is dropped and you ask again.

The numbers

The big number on the first page is the minutes you have left today. Every blocked app draws from it. Asking for 10 minutes takes 10 off at once, and closing the app early doesn't give them back. The minutes refill when down hours end, which is when Silk's day starts.

The second page, Mirror, scores your last full day out of 100. Higher is quieter. The score drops with every minute you unlock and every time you run into a blocked app's screen, and those run-ins count double during down hours. On your first day it shows today so far.

The squares under the score are the week, darker for a better day. The number beside the key is how many times you unlocked an app today. The leaves round the edge fill in a little more for every day you've had Silk.

Down hours

Overnight, nothing opens, whatever minutes are left. A blocked app's screen shows when down hours end.

Changing the rules

Anything stricter applies at once. Anything looser, like a bigger budget or shorter down hours, waits until tomorrow. Silk shows what's waiting, such as "Tomorrow: 60", with an "Apply now." button if you want it today.

Settings, the third page, holds your budget, down hours and apps. You can also write changes in the bar:

  • block TikTok until 9
  • close everything
  • set my budget to 30
  • cap Instagram at 20 (Instagram gets at most 20 of the day's minutes)
  • uncap Instagram
  • down hours at 11

Silk sends no notifications and keeps no streaks. Other questions are answered on the support page.
